# Debt-Schedule Mechanics for Institutional-Grade Annual-Period LBO Models: Conventions, Alternatives, and Reviewer Flags

## TL;DR
- **The dominant annual-model conventions are:** interest on the **average of opening/closing balance** (with a circularity toggle), a **credit-agreement-defined ECF sweep** (EBITDA-based build, 75/50/25/0% leverage step-downs), a revolver as the **cash plug**, **max(base rate, floor) + spread** pricing, **1% p.a. TLB amortization** with bullet, and **101 soft-call for 6 months** on TLBs. Sweeps and mandatory amortization are **exempt** from soft call.
- **The single most consequential US vs. European difference** is amortization/benchmark: US TLBs amortize at 1% p.a. on a Term SOFR base with a 0.00–0.50% floor; European TLBs frequently carry **zero scheduled amortization** (full bullet) on a EURIBOR base with a **0% (zero) floor**, and TLA "pro-rata" structures (heavy amortization, maintenance covenants) remain more common in Europe.
- **Reviewers flag** any of: interest on a static beginning balance without disclosure, a sweep that ignores the minimum-cash floor or senior/revolver priority, floors modeled as additive rather than as a `MAX`, OID treated as a cash use twice, and mandatory amortization computed off the beginning balance rather than original face.

## Key Findings
Institutional practice is remarkably standardized on the *mechanics* even as the *legal definitions* (especially ECF) are deal-specific. The named practitioner canon — Rosenbaum & Pearl, Macabacus, Wall Street Prep, Multiple Expansion, Breaking Into Wall Street, CFI — converges on the same modeling shortcuts, while the LSTA/LMA and law-firm sources (Skadden, Clifford Chance, Sidley, White & Case) govern the credit-agreement reality the model approximates. The eight items below give the standard convention, the live alternatives, the source attribution, and the reviewer flag.

### Item 1 — Interest calculation in an annual model (§4)

Interest expense in an institutional annual LBO model is standardly computed on the **average of the opening and closing balance** of each tranche, multiplied by the period rate, because principal is amortized/swept during the year and the average better approximates the true time-weighted balance. This deliberately creates a circular reference (interest → net income → cash → debt paydown → average balance → interest), which is managed with a **circularity toggle / "circuit breaker"** that flips the calculation to the **beginning balance** (or zero) to clear errors, with Excel iterative calculation enabled.

| Convention | Alternatives | Citation | Reviewer-flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interest = **average(beginning, ending balance) × rate**, with an IF-toggle "circ breaker" (e.g., `=IF(Circ=1, AVERAGE(beg,end)*rate, beg*rate)`) and Excel iterative calc (100 iterations, max change 0.001) | (a) **Beginning-of-period balance only** — no circularity, marginally conservative (slightly overstates interest), used by some banks that ban circular refs for stability; (b) **copy-paste macro** to hardcode then refresh; (c) **algebraic/UDF solution** (Bodmer) solving the sweep in closed form; (d) quarterly/semi-annual sub-schedule off prior-period ending balance to avoid circularity while capturing intra-year paydown | Macabacus, *Debt Characteristics* / *LBO Model Introduction* ("Interest is normally computed on average balances… eliminate circularity… by toggling off average interest with a 'circuit breaker'"); Wall Street Prep, *Debt Schedule* (circuit-breaker "Circ" cell: 1=average, 0=zero); Multiple Expansion, *Training LBO* (CHOOSE/IF switch: `if(CIRC=1, average(...), prior year balance)`); Edward Bodmer, *Circular References in Corporate Finance* (UDF/algebra); Rosenbaum & Pearl, *Investment Banking* (illustrative LBO, interest on average TLB balance) | Interest hardcoded to a **static beginning balance without disclosure**; iterative calc left off (model #DIV/0! or #VALUE! errors); circ toggle wired so it silently zeroes interest in the live case; average-balance interest applied but sweep timing ignored |

**Day-count / annual-approximation nuance (cannot fit in table):** Real leveraged loans accrue interest on an **Actual/360** basis (Term SOFR loans), which produces ~1.4% more interest than the nominal rate (365/360) and more than a 30/360 bond basis; high-yield bonds use **30/360**. An annual model that multiplies an annual coupon by an average balance implicitly assumes 30/360-style even accrual and **understates cash interest versus Actual/360 reality by roughly 1.0–1.4%** of the interest figure, and separately biases the result because it assumes smooth mid-year paydown rather than the lumpy quarterly Actual/360 accrual on a declining balance. Institutional models generally accept this as immaterial "false precision" at the annual grain; where precision matters, modelers either apply an Actual/360 gross-up factor (rate × 365/360) or drop to a quarterly schedule. A reviewer expects the day-count basis to at least be **stated per tranche** even if the annual model does not literally compute daily accruals.

### Item 2 — Excess Cash Flow (ECF) sweep (§2, §5)

The ECF sweep is a **mandatory prepayment** of term loans equal to a percentage of a contractually defined cash-flow pool. The legal build (LSTA-style credit agreement) typically starts from **Consolidated Net Income or Adjusted EBITDA** and adds/subtracts a defined list; the model simplifies this to a free-cash-flow proxy. Sweep percentages **step down with leverage**, most commonly **50%/25%/0%** or **75%/50%/25%/0%** at defined first-lien/total leverage thresholds.

| Convention | Alternatives | Citation | Reviewer-flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pool build (legal):** start with EBITDA (or net income), **subtract** cash interest, cash taxes, mandatory debt amortization, capex (actual or capped), and increases in working capital; adjust for other defined items. **Sweep %:** step-down grid, e.g. **75% (>threshold) → 50% → 25% → 0%**, tested on leverage ratio. **Sequencing:** revolver repaid first, then first-lien TLB (pro rata across term lenders), then junior. **Modeling shortcut:** sweep = a % of (FCF after mandatory amort − minimum cash + beginning excess cash) | (a) Net-income-based vs. EBITDA-based build; (b) two-tier (100%/50% at one leverage test) vs. three/four-tier grids; (c) capex deducted at **actual** vs. **capped/budgeted**; (d) whether **balance-sheet cash above the minimum floor** enters the pool (often it accumulates on the B/S rather than being swept once minimum cash is met); (e) 100% "full sweep" simplification in quick models; (f) "starter/builder basket" and de-minimis threshold carve-outs; (g) prepayment applied to reduce amortization pro rata vs. in direct order of maturity | Wall Street Prep, *LBO Cash Sweep* (mandatory amort on original principal, then sweep of remaining excess above minimum cash, MIN function floor at zero); Breaking Into Wall Street, *Cash Flow Sweep* (CFADR = Beginning Cash + FCF − Minimum Cash; revolver → TLA → TLB priority; sweep % applied after senior repayments); Cummings Law, *Structuring ECF Sweeps* ("75 percent sweep when net leverage exceeds a defined ratio, stepping down to 50 percent and then 25 percent"; capex deducted at actual vs. capped changes the result); Lexology/Skadden (Applicable Prepayment Percentage step-downs; pro rata across term tranches; ECF sweep standard in TLB agreements); Law Insider ECF clauses (e.g., "75% of Excess Cash Flow… 50%… if Total Leverage ≤1.50:1.00… 25% if ≤1.00:1.00") | Sweep computed **before** the minimum-cash reservation; capex deduction basis (actual vs. capped) not matched to the agreement; step-down leverage test using sponsor-add-back EBITDA never actually reached; sweep applied to junior debt ahead of first-lien; **voluntary prepayments not credited** against the ECF requirement (real agreements deduct voluntary paydowns); cash swept even when the credit agreement lets excess accumulate on the balance sheet |

**Nuance:** In a WSO practitioner example, a 50% TLB sweep on $40 of ECF with a $100 opening balance pays down only $20 and the remaining $20 **accumulates on the balance sheet** even with debt outstanding — the standard interpretation is "% of cash flow available," not "% of remaining balance." Reviewers watch which interpretation the modeler used because it materially changes deleveraging speed.

### Item 3 — Revolver mechanics in an annual model (§3, §5)

The revolver is the model's **cash plug**: drawn automatically when cash would otherwise fall below the minimum balance, and repaid first (ahead of term-loan sweeps) when cash is available. It is the most senior instrument, so any outstanding balance is minimized before optional term-loan repayment.

| Convention | Alternatives | Citation | Reviewer-flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Draw-to-floor / repay-from-excess:** draw = MAX(0, minimum cash − pre-financing cash); repay = MIN(beginning revolver balance, available cash above minimum). Interest on drawn balance uses **average balance** (same circ toggle as Item 1). **Commitment/undrawn fee** = fixed rate × (facility limit − average drawn amount). Intra-year seasonality is **ignored** — the annual model assumes a single net draw/repay | (a) Interest on **beginning drawn balance** to avoid extra circularity; (b) explicit borrowing-base/ABL cap where relevant; (c) commitment fee on **average undrawn** vs. period-end undrawn; (d) quarterly/13-week sub-model for working-capital-intensive or seasonal businesses to capture peak draws the annual model misses | Macabacus, *Interest Rate Assumptions* / *Capital Structure of an LBO* ("two costs… the interest rate charged on the revolver's drawn balance, and an undrawn commitment fee… fixed rate multiplied by the difference between the revolver's limit and any drawn amount"); Breaking Into Wall Street, *Debt Schedule* (draw on revolver via MIN function when cash insufficient; repay first); Wall Street Oasis, *Automatically Draw on a Revolver* (CFADS build; revolver auto-draws shortfall, cash never dips below minimum); Rosenbaum & Pearl (Revolving Credit Facility in transaction structure / debt schedule) | Revolver not repaid **first** before term sweeps; cash allowed to fall below the minimum (no auto-draw); revolver drawn beyond its facility limit/borrowing base; commitment fee omitted or charged on the drawn (not undrawn) portion; seasonal peak working-capital needs invisible in an annual model that should be quarterly |

### Item 4 — Benchmark / interest rate floors (§4)

The market-standard formula is **max(base rate, floor) + spread** — the floor applies to the *base rate* (Term SOFR/EURIBOR) before the margin is added, and is modeled as a `MAX`, never an addition. Floors are near-universal in loan documentation but are **currently non-binding** because Term SOFR (which stood in the ~3.6–4.3% range through 2025, per FRED/New York Fed) sits far above typical floor levels.

| Convention | Alternatives | Citation | Reviewer-flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rate = MAX(Term SOFR, floor) + margin.** US BSL TLBs: floor of **0.00% or 0.50%** is the current new-issue standard. EU TLBs: **0.00% (zero) floor** standard (LMA optional zero-floor wording). Base rate = **CME Term SOFR** (1/3/6-mo) post-LIBOR; **CSA largely dropped** in new-issue BSL (or embedded in spread) | (a) Legacy/private-credit floors of **0.75%–1.00%** (common pre-2022 and still in illiquid private-credit deals); (b) residual **credit-spread adjustment (CSA)** of ~10 bps flat or the ARRC curve (11.448/26.161/42.826 bps) on legacy/tough-legacy contracts; (c) fixed-rate tranches (sub debt, HY notes) with no base-rate/floor mechanic; (d) EURIBOR/SONIA/SARON bases for non-USD tranches | Morgan Stanley IM, *Floating-Rate Loan Market Monitor Q4 2025* (LCD-sourced): "Floors are a minimum base rate to be paid by a borrower before the fixed credit spread… Floor Benefit 0.00%"; Law Insider Term SOFR clauses ("the greater of (i) the Term SOFR Reference Rate… and (ii) the Floor"); First Trust NPORT-P SEC filings 2024–2025 (Clarivate 2024 TLB "SOFR + 2.75%, 0.00% Floor"; Consilio refi "SOFR+3.75%, 0.50% Floor"); CME Group, Term SOFR page ("USED BY OVER 2,870 FIRMS GLOBALLY, CME TERM SOFR IS THE PREFERRED CHOICE FOR USD LOANS QUOTED IN 1, 3, 6 AND 12 MONTH TENORS"); LMA zero-floor wording (AKD; ACT *Negative benchmarks in Europe*); White & Case (CSA largely absent in new-issue BSL by Q3 2022) | Floor **added** to the rate instead of applied as MAX (double-counts when SOFR < floor); floor applied to the all-in rate rather than the base rate; a 0.75%/1.00% floor assumed for a current BSL deal (overstates interest — that is a legacy/private-credit level); LIBOR still referenced as the base (LIBOR ceased **June 30, 2023**); CSA hardcoded on a new-issue deal that prices SOFR-flat |

**Transition nuance:** ARRC recommended CME's forward-looking Term SOFR on **July 29, 2021**; USD LIBOR ceased **June 30, 2023**; the LIBOR Act (Mar 2022) and Fed final rule (Dec 2022) migrated tough-legacy contracts to Term SOFR + ARRC CSAs, **retaining any existing floor (now as a Term SOFR floor).** A current-dated model should use Term SOFR as the base and treat any floor as economically inert at today's rates while still modeling it as a MAX for downside/rate-cut scenarios.

### Item 5 — Original Issue Discount (OID) (§3, Sources & Uses)

For **modeling** purposes OID is treated identically to a capitalized financing fee: amortized on a **straight-line** basis over the loan term (accounting reality is the **effective-interest method**, but straight-line is the accepted shortcut when immaterial), added back as a non-cash item, and **written off in full on prepayment/refinancing**. In Sources & Uses, OID reduces net cash proceeds to the borrower, so it is shown either as debt raised **net of OID** (source = 99) or **gross with OID as a use** (source = 100, use = 1).

| Convention | Alternatives | Citation | Reviewer-flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Modeling:** OID lumped with financing fees; **straight-line amortization** over tenor into interest expense (non-cash add-back); **remaining unamortized OID accelerated (written off) on prepayment/refi.** **S&U:** either (a) debt as a source **net of OID** (99), or (b) full face as source (100) with OID as a use (1); interest/amort/PIK always computed on **full face (100)** | (a) **Effective-interest method** (GAAP-correct; interest method required unless straight-line is immaterially different); (b) bullet accretion at maturity (zero-coupon style); (c) OID shown as a **contra-liability** on the B/S vs. netted silently against debt; (d) fair-value election (ASC 825) expensing OID immediately | Wall Street Prep, *OID* (amortized over term; B/S accretes to par; "if the bond is repaid early… the issuing firm recognizes a loss"); Wall Street Prep, *Advanced LBO Modeling Test* ("financing fees and OID will be treated identically… lump this $21mm into the total financing fees"); Macabacus, *Original Issue Discount* (effective interest; early-repayment loss); Breaking Into Wall Street, *Sources & Uses* / OID slides ("company still pays interest based on the face value"); Investment Banking Analysts, *S&U Table* ("1,000 face… 2% OID… yields 970 cash if netted… the 30 nets against debt and amortizes to interest expense"); balancedsolutions.pro (interest/effective method for OID, straight-line if not materially different) | Interest computed on the **net (99)** balance instead of face (100); OID **double-counted** (shown net in sources AND as a use); unamortized OID **not written off** on early repayment/refi; OID capitalized as an asset that never unwinds; effective-interest vs. straight-line mismatch that distorts early-year interest on a large discount |

### Item 6 — Call protection (§3)

Institutional TLBs carry a **101 soft call** (1% premium) for a limited window — **6 months** is the current US standard, though **12–18 months** appears in some broadly syndicated deals — applying **only to repricing/refinancing** transactions, **not** to ordinary voluntary paydowns, mandatory amortization, or ECF sweeps. High-yield notes instead carry **hard-call** protection: a non-call period (typically half the tenor) with make-whole, then a **declining premium schedule** starting at par + 50% of coupon.

| Convention | Alternatives | Citation | Reviewer-flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TLB:** **101 soft call for 6 months** on repricing/refinancing only; **sweeps and mandatory amortization are exempt.** LBO models often **ignore** soft call (window short, sweeps exempt) but include HY **make-whole/call premia** at exit if refinancing within the non-call period. **HY notes:** e.g. 5NC2/8NC3; make-whole during non-call; then premium par+50%/par+25%/par declining ratably (e.g., 106/104/102/100). Equity claw ~35–40% at par+coupon; 101 change-of-control put | (a) **12–18 month** soft-call windows (older/BSL norm); (b) private-credit **102/101 hard call** — a 2% premium if prepayment occurs on or before the first anniversary of closing and 1% if after the first but on or before the second anniversary (Sidley), applying to voluntary + certain mandatory prepayments; (c) make-whole for the entire term ("NC-life"); (d) modeling exits that simply assume bonds are refinanced at par (ignoring premia) vs. explicitly adding make-whole to exit costs | Law Insider *Call Protection* / *Soft Call Protection* ("soft call provision at 101%… first 6 months… only to voluntary prepayments of Facility B"; repricing-transaction 1.00% premium); Termgrid (BSL "101 soft call for the first 12–18 months"); Sidley Austin, *Understanding Call Protection in Private Credit* (Aug 2025): "Historically, six-month soft call protection was limited to the broadly syndicated TLB market, where institutional investors often did not intend to hold the debt to maturity"; and on hard call: "typically structured as '102/101 hard call'"; Clifford Chance, *How Soft is Your Soft Call?* (repricing-transaction definition, exemptions); PitchBook *High-Yield Bond Primer* (non-call = half term; par+50% coupon declining); Termgrid *Call Premium* (5NC2: 50%/25%/0%); Latham *Bond Mechanics 201* (make-whole T+50) | Soft-call premium wrongly applied to **mandatory amortization or ECF sweeps** (they are exempt); a 6-month soft call modeled as if it lasts years; **HY make-whole ignored** when the model refinances/exits inside the non-call period; private-credit hard call omitted on an illiquid tranche; change-of-control 101 put ignored in a sale scenario |

### Item 7 — Mandatory amortization standards by instrument (§3, tranche templates)

The US institutional standard is **TLB at 1% per annum** (0.25% quarterly) with a **99% bullet** at maturity; **TLA** amortizes heavily (bank/"pro-rata" market); high-yield notes, second lien, and sub/mezz are **bullet** (no amortization). Mandatory amortization is computed on **original face value**, not the declining balance.

| Convention | Alternatives | Citation | Reviewer-flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TLB (US institutional):** **1.0% p.a. (0.25%/qtr)**, bullet ~99% at maturity (7–7.5 yr tenor). **TLA (bank/pro-rata):** heavy amortization — commonly **~5–10% p.a.**, ranging up to **15–25%** in some structures, fully amortizing over 5–6 yr, and typically carrying a **financial maintenance covenant** (White & Case). **HY notes / 2nd lien / sub / mezz:** **bullet**, no scheduled amortization. Amort based on **original principal** (`-MIN(Original × %, Original)`), floored at zero | (a) **European TLB:** frequently **0% scheduled amortization** (full bullet), even more bullet-weighted than US; (b) TLA more prevalent in Europe and in the "pro-rata" bank market — per White & Case *Debt Explorer*, in H1 2022 pro-rata tranches were **67.31% of overall US leveraged loan issuance (up from 35.15% in 2021)** and **half of Western & Southern Europe issuance (up from 43.8% in 2021)**; (c) sculpted/step-up amortization; (d) some senior debt modeled at 5% in teaching examples; (e) 0% mandatory amort assumed in quick tests unless specified | Macabacus, *Debt Amortization* (TLA vs TLB amortization; % of original face); Wall Street Prep, *Debt Schedule* (5% senior amort on original principal; `-MIN(...)`); White & Case *Debt Explorer*, *Term loan A debt is back in fashion* ("TLB… amortizes at just 1% a year"; TLA "meaningful amortization… 5 years"; "pro rata facilities will typically include a financial maintenance covenant"); De Brauw / Termgrid / VectorShift (TLB "1% per annum, if any… bullet"; "no amortization in the European Leveraged Loan market"); Ryan O'Connell (TLA "15-25% annually"; TLB "1% annually… 94-96% bullet"); LSTA overview (institutional TLB structural features); Rosenbaum & Pearl (Term Loan A/B amortization in financing structure) | TLB amortization computed off the **beginning balance** instead of original face (understates late-year amort as balance declines from sweeps); European TLB modeled with 1% amort when the deal is a **full bullet**; TLA modeled as bullet; HY/sub debt given amortization it doesn't have; ending balance allowed to go **negative** (missing MIN/zero floor) |

### Item 8 — Commitment/undrawn fees and agency fees (§4, §5)

The fee that always belongs in an LBO model is the **revolver undrawn commitment fee**; larger deals also carry an **administrative agency fee** and, for delayed-draw/acquisition facilities, a **ticking fee**. The standard modeling question is where each hits for **DSCR**: interest-type fees (commitment, ticking) sit in the **interest/finance-cost line** (and thus in debt service), while the admin agency fee is small and typically lumped into the fee/interest line rather than modeled separately.

| Convention | Alternatives | Citation | Reviewer-flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Include:** revolver **undrawn commitment fee** (fixed % × average undrawn) in the interest-expense/finance-cost line — flows into DSCR debt service. **Amortize** capitalized financing fees/OID as non-cash interest. Agency fee: small, usually folded into fees/interest. **Ticking fee** on committed-but-undrawn acquisition/DDTL capacity where relevant. Granularity beyond this (separate lines for L/C fees, admin fee) is usually **overkill** for an annual LBO | (a) Commitment fee as a **separate fees line** vs. inside interest expense; (b) financing-fee amortization run through **D&A** rather than interest (changes EBIT but not EBITDA); (c) explicit **letter-of-credit fees** and **admin agent fees** in highly detailed lender models; (d) in project-finance/DSCR-sculpted models, commitment fee and interest included in **CFADS**/debt-service precisely; (e) ignoring commitment fee entirely in quick tests | Macabacus, *Interest Rate Assumptions* (undrawn commitment fee line; "fixed rate multiplied by the difference between the revolver's limit and any drawn amount"); Wall Street Oasis, *Treatment of fees in LBO* (financing-fee amortization in interest line vs. D&A; revolver commitment fee capitalize/amortize question; "Revolver… used in the debt-service-coverage-ratio"); Investment Banking Analysts, *S&U Table* ("recurring costs include interest, undrawn commitment fees, letter-of-credit fees, and admin agent fees"); Edward Bodmer (commitment fee and interest included in CFADS for DSCR); Breaking Into Wall Street, *DSCR* (only **scheduled** debt service counts; optional sweeps excluded); CFI *Revolver Debt* (commitment fee mechanics) | Commitment fee **omitted** (understates cash cost and DSCR); financing-fee amortization double-counted as both cash fee at close and recurring cash interest; fee placed inconsistently (interest vs. D&A) so **EBIT/coverage ratios don't tie**; optional sweep prepayments wrongly included in the **DSCR debt-service denominator** (only scheduled service counts); over-engineered fee granularity that adds no return/covenant insight |

## Details
**On circularity philosophy (Item 1):** the practitioner community is split but leans average-balance. The buy-side/PE canon (Rosenbaum & Pearl illustrative model, Macabacus, Wall Street Prep, Multiple Expansion) teaches average balance with a toggle as the "correct" method; a meaningful minority of banks mandate beginning-balance calculation to eliminate circularity entirely, accepting a small conservative bias. Both are defensible; what a reviewer will not accept is an **undisclosed** choice or a broken toggle.

**On ECF legal-vs-model gap (Item 2):** the credit-agreement definition is heavily negotiated and, per Cummings Law and Skadden, "customary" varies by market segment. The model should mirror the agreement line-by-line where one exists; in a pre-LOI or generic model, the EBITDA-based build with a 75/50/25/0% grid tested on first-lien net leverage is the defensible default. Voluntary prepayments and reinvested asset-sale proceeds typically **reduce** the required ECF sweep — a subtlety most quick models omit.

**On regional differences (Items 4 & 7):** the two cleanest US/Europe splits are (1) **benchmark and floor** — Term SOFR + 0.00/0.50% floor (US) vs. EURIBOR + 0% zero-floor (Europe, LMA wording); and (2) **amortization** — 1% p.a. US TLB vs. frequently **zero-amortization** European TLB, with TLA/pro-rata structures more entrenched in Europe (pro-rata reached 67.31% of US and ~50% of Western & Southern European leveraged-loan issuance in H1 2022, per White & Case). European TLB documentation has also converged toward US high-yield-style incurrence covenants and "grower" (vs. "builder") baskets, but pro-rata/TLA facilities typically retain a financial maintenance covenant and tighter debt/restricted-payment capacity.

## Recommendations
Staged, concrete steps for the spec:
1. **§4 (interest rates) — adopt average-balance with a mandatory circ toggle** and Excel iterative calc as the default; require the day-count basis (Actual/360 for SOFR loans, 30/360 for notes) to be **stated per tranche** and, if precision matters at the annual grain, apply an Actual/360 gross-up (rate × 365/360). Benchmark = `MAX(Term SOFR, floor) + margin`, floors defaulted to **0.00–0.50% (US) / 0.00% (Europe)**. *Threshold to revisit:* if a live credit agreement specifies otherwise, or if forward SOFR is modeled below ~1%, the floor becomes binding and must be tested.
2. **§3 (debt waterfall) & tranche templates — hardcode instrument defaults:** TLB 1% p.a. on original face + bullet (US) / 0% (Europe); TLA 5–10%+; notes/2nd-lien/mezz bullet; MIN-function floor at zero; OID lumped with fees, straight-line amortized, written off on prepay; interest/amort always on face (100). Soft call 101/6-months, **exempting sweeps and mandatory amort**; include HY make-whole in exit cases inside the non-call period.
3. **§5 (waterfall sequencing) — enforce strict priority:** (i) mandatory amortization → (ii) reserve minimum cash → (iii) revolver repayment → (iv) first-lien TLB ECF sweep (% grid on leverage) → (v) junior → (vi) residual to balance-sheet cash. Credit voluntary prepayments against the ECF requirement. Put the commitment fee in the finance-cost line and count only **scheduled** service (not optional sweeps) in DSCR.
4. **Reviewer checklist:** build the flag column above into a QA tab — Sources = Uses to the penny, balance sheet balances every period, no negative debt balances, no cash below minimum, floors as MAX not addition, OID not double-counted.

*Benchmarks that would change the recommendation:* a specific executed credit agreement overrides all generic defaults; a WC-intensive/seasonal target justifies dropping from annual to quarterly to capture peak revolver draws; a private-credit (not BSL) financing shifts floors to 0.75–1.00% and call protection to 102/101 hard call.

## Caveats
- **ECF has no universal formula** — every credit agreement differs; the grids and build shown are market-typical defaults, not law. Where an agreement exists, model it verbatim.
- **Floor prevalence is not backed by a free named percentage** — the dedicated search could not retrieve a public LCD/Covenant Review/Xtract statistic for the *share* of TLBs carrying a floor; the 0.00%/0.50% conclusion is inferred from named SEC deal filings (First Trust NPORT-P) and Morgan Stanley IM's LCD-sourced "floor benefit = 0.00%" data. For a headline prevalence figure, consult PitchBook LCD, Covenant Review, or Xtract Research directly.
- **Forward-looking item:** CME's free cash-market licensing for Term SOFR runs "up to the end of 2026" (per White & Case) — a status to monitor, not a completed event.
- The Term SOFR level range (~3.6–4.3% through 2025) is point-in-time and drawn from FRED/New York Fed series; it was not independently re-verified in a live-rate table, and the floor's economic relevance flips if base rates fall materially.
- Several supporting citations are practitioner forums (Wall Street Oasis) and secondary blogs; these corroborate the canonical sources (Macabacus, WSP, Rosenbaum & Pearl, LSTA/law firms) but should not be the sole authority for a spec.

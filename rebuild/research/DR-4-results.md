# LBO Market Data Points for Modeling-Tool Defaults (2024–2026)

## TL;DR
- **As of mid-2026, large-cap broadly syndicated (BSL) LBOs default to ~5.0x total leverage / ~4.9x first-lien, SOFR+~450–475 pricing, ~46% equity, and 12.0–12.5x entry EV/EBITDA; middle-market/direct-lending deals default to ~4.0x total debt, ~3.1x senior, unitranche at SOFR+425–525, and ~7.2x entry multiples** — every figure below carries a specific, verifiable citation with an as-of date.
- **Trends 2024→2025→2026:** leverage crept modestly higher off 2023–24 lows; new-issue spreads compressed through 2025 then began widening 25–50 bps in Q1 2026; equity contributions eased from a 2023 peak of 51.1% to 46%; cov-lite stayed >90% in BSL while private credit largely retained maintenance covenants.
- **Use conservative modeling conventions confirmed by market practice:** exit multiple = entry multiple (flat), 50% ECF sweep with leverage-based step-downs, springing revolver covenant at 35–40% draw, 1% annual TLB amortization, and hold periods now ~7 years at exit.

## Key Findings
The single most decision-relevant fact: LBO leverage in 2024–2026 is structurally lower, and equity checks structurally higher, than the 2021 peak — driven by higher base rates squeezing interest coverage (which hit record lows of ~2.3x in 2024). Large-cap BSL total leverage sits around 5.0x (down from 5.9x in 2022), first-lien debt is doing almost all the work (~4.9x, a record) with negligible second-lien, and sponsor equity contributions run in the mid-40s percent versus a ~41% pre-2022 norm. Middle-market deals are materially less levered (~4.0x total, ~3.1x senior) and priced higher on an all-in basis. Base rates have fallen meaningfully: overnight SOFR was 3.66% on July 1, 2026 and the 90-day average SOFR was ~3.64% as of late June 2026 (Federal Reserve Bank of New York), down from the 5.33% peak of September 2023 — a tailwind that is expected to push leverage gently higher and equity gently lower over the coming quarters.

### CATEGORY 5 — EXCESS CASH FLOW (ECF) SWEEP

| Item | Standard | Citation | As-of date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base ECF sweep | 50% | LSTA cash sweep market data (via CT Acquisitions LBO guide) | 2025 |
| Step-down grid (conventional sponsor deal) | 75% above ~4.5x leverage → 50% at 3.5–4.5x → 0% below 3.5x | LSTA cash sweep market data (via CT Acquisitions) | 2025 |
| Alternative grid (leverage-based, common form) | 50% base → 25% at lower leverage band → 0% at tighter threshold | Cummings Law, "Understanding Cash Sweeps in LBOs" | 2025 |
| Illustrative credit-agreement grid | 75% ECF → 50% (leverage ≤1.5x) → 25% (leverage ≤1.0x) | Law Insider, Excess Cash Flow Sweep sample clauses (real filings) | 2025 |

**Recommended default for the tool:** **50% base ECF sweep** with step-downs to 25% and 0% as leverage declines; set an "aggressive/lender-friendly" preset that starts at 75% above a ~4.5x threshold, stepping to 50% (3.5–4.5x) and 0% (<3.5x). Model ECF as EBITDA − cash interest − mandatory amortization − cash taxes − capex ± working-capital change, applied first to the term loan.

| Share held >5 years | ~40% (up from 29% in 2019) | Bain Global PE Report 2026: "Almost 40% of all companies are now held for more than five years, up from 29% in 2019 (see Figure 17)." | 2025 |
| Exit multiple convention (IC discipline) | Exit multiple = entry multiple (flat) | Wall Street Prep, Macabacus, CT Acquisitions: industry best practice sets exit = entry; multiple expansion "usually an unjustifiable assumption" | 2025–26 |

**Rationale:** Setting exit = entry removes unearned "multiple expansion" from the return, isolating operational value creation (EBITDA growth) and debt paydown as the return drivers. Since 2022, higher rates, slower distributions, and skeptical buyers have reduced the premium exits command over held-asset marks, reinforcing the flat-exit convention. Downside cases commonly haircut entry by 0.5–1.0x; upside cases test +1.0–2.0x — but the **base-case default should be flat (exit = entry)**.

## Recommendations
1. **Adopt segment-aware defaults.** Detect whether the deal is large-cap/BSL (EV >$1B) or middle-market, and switch defaults accordingly:
   - *Large-cap / BSL:* 5.0x total / 4.9x first-lien leverage; 46% equity; SOFR+450–475 pricing (0% floor, OID 99.5); 11–12.5x entry EV/EBITDA.
   - *Middle-market / direct lending:* 4.0x total / 3.1x senior; ~50% equity; unitranche SOFR+475–525 (2–3% OID); 7.2x entry EV/EBITDA (5.9x for sub-$25M, 10.0x for $100–250M).
2. **Hard-code conservative conventions:** exit multiple = entry multiple; 50% base ECF sweep with 75/50/25/0 step-down grid; 1% annual TLB amortization with bullet at maturity; springing revolver covenant at 40% draw with 30–35% EBITDA headroom; 7-year hold in the base/sensitivity case.
3. **Surface as-of dates and refresh quarterly.** The most time-sensitive inputs are new-issue spreads, base rate (SOFR), and equity contributions — refresh from PitchBook LCD Credit Markets Weekly/Quarterly Wrap and GF Data quarterly M&A/Leverage Reports.
4. **Thresholds that should change the defaults:** if 3M SOFR falls sustainably below ~3.5%, raise leverage defaults toward 5.5–6.0x (large-cap) and 4.5–5.0x (MM) and lower equity below 45%; if new-issue spreads widen >50 bps from current levels, raise pricing defaults and lower leverage; if BSL cov-lite share moves materially off ~90%, revisit the covenant preset.

## Caveats
- Several 2026 data points reflect a specific, event-driven macro backdrop — PitchBook cites a US–Iran military conflict in early 2026 and an AI-driven reassessment of software credit quality that widened spreads and slowed deal flow in Q1 2026. Treat these as event-driven, not structural.
- Sector-level entry multiples are the weakest-sourced category (see Category 8 caveats); treat them as directional and blended (NA+Europe, PE+corporate).
- Some pricing/fee ranges (mezzanine, unitranche OID, arrangement fees) draw on practitioner/market-convention sources rather than a single index; they are corroborated across multiple sources but are ranges, not index readings.
- Where PitchBook reports two "buyout" series (US-geography vs NA+Europe global), the figures differ by ~0.5 turn — always cite which geography scope is being displayed.
- "Midpoint suggestions" are the author's recommended single default for a UI, not published figures.
- The 2025 Annual US PE Breakdown carries an internal date inconsistency (contents page prints "January 13, 2025" against "As of December 31, 2025" data and a "© 2026" copyright) — the correct publication is January 2026.

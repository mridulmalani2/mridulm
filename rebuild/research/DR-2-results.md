# Deal-Level LBO Return Calculations and Fund Metrics: A Reviewer-Ready Reference

**Scope:** Deal-level LBO model (sponsor equity IRR / MOIC), with fund-level overlay noted where it changes the answer. Confidence tags: HARD (well-established convention / primary source), ESTIMATE (derived or approximate), REPORTED (market survey figure).

---

## TL;DR
- The dominant deal-level convention funds the sponsor's t=0 equity check with the purchase equity value plus transaction (advisory) fees, financing fees, and OID, all sitting in Uses with equity as the plug; rollover equity reduces the sponsor's required check (it is both a source and, economically, a non-cash offset). Label your pre-carry return series "pre-promote IRR" rather than "gross IRR": it is defensible and more precise, provided you define it, because ILPA and GIPS reserve "gross" for a fund/portfolio concept (before management fees and carry).
- The single largest reviewer trap across items is convention-mixing: a mid-year exit shifted to t−0.5 while the debt schedule accrues a full year of exit-year interest; a US promote waterfall layered on top of a UK sweet-equity cap table (double count); and monitoring-fee income counted twice (once as sponsor inflow, once ignored as an FCF drag).
- For value-creation bridges, reconcile to pre-promote equity first (management incentive is a distribution of value, not a source of it); present the EBITDA-growth × multiple-change cross term explicitly rather than silently burying it.

## Item 1: T=0 Sponsor Equity Outflow Composition

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Convention** | HARD. In the Sources & Uses, **Uses** = purchase equity value (or enterprise value less assumed debt) + **buy-side transaction/advisory fees** + **financing fees** + **OID** + minimum cash / refinanced target debt. **Sources** = new debt (at face, with OID shown separately) + rollover equity + excess balance-sheet cash + **sponsor equity as the plug**. Because fees and OID sit in Uses, they increase the sponsor's equity check dollar-for-dollar unless funded by an incremental debt draw. Accounting split (post-2015 US GAAP, ASU 2015-03): **transaction/advisory fees are expensed** (hit Day-1 equity/retained earnings); **financing fees on term debt/bonds are a contra-liability** amortized into interest expense over the debt's life; **financing fees on revolvers are capitalized as an asset** and amortized; **OID is a contra-liability** accreted into interest expense (treated identically to financing fees in most models). Rollover equity **reduces the sponsor's cash equity contribution**: management contributes its rolled proceeds as a non-cash source, so the sponsor's required check falls by the rollover amount, and sponsor MOIC/IRR is computed on the sponsor-only check. |
| **Alternatives** | (a) **Fees financed by debt**: on some deals transaction expenses are funded by an incremental revolver/term draw or added to debt principal rather than the equity check, keeping the equity check smaller (common where a delayed-draw or upsized facility exists). (b) **Enterprise-value-funded vs. equity-funded fees**: fees may be "capitalized into entry EV" in simplified/paper models, which raises the effective entry multiple. (c) **Sponsor deal fee to itself**: a transaction fee paid to the sponsor at close can be a Use that partly returns to the sponsor (see Item 5). (d) **Cash-free/debt-free (CFDF) deals**: target cash and debt are excluded, changing the bridge from EV to equity value. (e) **Rollover as pure source without netting**: if rollover exceeds fees/other uses, the model may cap sources and treat the sponsor check as the residual, occasionally producing a "sources exceed uses" artifact that must be reconciled. |
| **Citations** | Rosenbaum & Pearl, *Investment Banking* (LBO Analysis chapter), "Sources and Uses" and the pro forma balance-sheet adjustments (fee capitalization vs. expensing). Macabacus LBO model, "Transaction & Financing Fees" (advisory fee as % of transaction size; financing fee as % of each debt tranche; miscellaneous fixed legal/accounting; possible sponsor fee) and stub-period/amortization treatment. Wall Street Prep S&U guidance (OID embedded in Uses raises the sponsor equity contribution; OID and financing fees "treated identically"). FASB ASU 2015-03 (debt issuance costs as a direct deduction from the carrying amount of debt). |
| **Reviewer-flag** | (1) **Did fees get double-funded?** If fees are in Uses AND netted from debt proceeds, the equity plug is wrong. (2) **OID vs. face value**: debt must be raised at face on Sources with OID separately in Uses; using net proceeds as the debt source understates required equity. (3) **Amortization location**: financing-fee/OID amortization belongs in interest expense (below EBIT), not in operating D&A — misplacing it distorts EBITDA and coverage ratios. (4) **Rollover netting**: confirm the MOIC denominator is the sponsor-only check, not total equity; confirm whether rollover holders participate pari passu at exit. (5) **Expensed transaction fees still consume cash at close** even though they never touch the P&L as amortization — a common cash-flow modeling error. (6) Revolver financing fees on the undrawn commitment are often forgotten. |

## Item 2: ILPA Return Metric Definitions and the "Gross" vs "Pre-Promote" Question

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Convention** | HARD. **ILPA definitions (fund level, LP cash-flow based):** **DPI** = cumulative distributions to LPs ÷ paid-in capital (the realized/"cash-back" multiple). **RVPI** = current fair value of remaining (unrealized) investments ÷ paid-in capital (the unrealized multiple). **TVPI** = (cumulative distributions + residual NAV) ÷ paid-in capital = **DPI + RVPI** (the total multiple). Denominator is **paid-in (drawn) capital**, not committed. Verbatim ILPA glossary: **Net IRR** = "the dollar-weighted internal rate of return, net of management fees and carried interest generated by an investment in the fund." **Gross IRR** = "the IRR based upon the performance of the investments, not taking into account management fees or carried interest." The ILPA 2025 Performance Template formalizes **fund-level** metrics (fund-to-LP cash flows) versus **portfolio-level / gross** metrics (fund-to-investment cash flows), and requires TVPI/IRR both **with and without** subscription-line impact. |
| **Alternatives** | **Deal level vs fund level:** at the single-deal level, DPI/RVPI/TVPI collapse to realized proceeds, residual value, and MOIC on the invested equity; the same ratios, but on one asset rather than a pooled fund. "Gross IRR" at the deal level legitimately means the return on invested equity **before fund-level management fees, fund expenses, and carried interest/promote**, but a deal model can define its own "before what" line. Legitimate deal-level return layers, from most to least gross: (1) **unlevered/asset IRR** (Item 6); (2) **levered pre-promote equity IRR** (before any management incentive/carry); (3) **post-promote / post-management-incentive equity IRR**; (4) **net-to-LP IRR** (after fund fees + carry — only meaningful with a fund overlay). GIPS (private equity provisions) requires **since-inception IRR (SI-IRR)**; gross-of-fees returns must be **net of transaction costs** but **before** carried interest and management fees; net-of-fees returns deduct management fees and carried interest. |
| **Citations** | ILPA Private Equity Glossary (Gross IRR, Net IRR, TVPI, RVPI, DPI, Management Fee Offsets definitions — quoted verbatim above). ILPA Quarterly Reporting Standards / Reporting Template (TVPI = current value of remaining investments + total distributions to LPs ÷ total LP contributions). ILPA Performance Template (Jan 2025): fund-level vs portfolio-level gross/net tables; subscription-line with/without; note that ILPA *recommends* rather than *requires* fund-level gross IRR/MOIC and does not require presentation of the gross calculation inputs. GIPS 2020 Standards for Firms + Private Equity Guidance Statement (SI-IRR required; gross = net of transaction costs, before carry and mgmt fee; net = after carry and mgmt fee; TVPI/DPI/RVPI/PIC multiples required). |
| **Reviewer-flag** | (1) **"Gross" ambiguity:** a reviewer will ask "gross of what?" ILPA/GIPS reserve "gross" for the *before-management-fee-and-carry* concept. Labeling the pre-carry deal series **"pre-promote IRR"** is *more* precise and defensible than "gross," provided you (a) state explicitly it is still net of transaction costs and portfolio-company-level fees, and (b) do not also call it "gross" elsewhere — inconsistent labeling is the actual failure mode. The reviewer's residual concern: "pre-promote" is non-standard vocabulary, so define it once, and be clear it is NOT net-of-fund-fee (it is not an LP return). (2) **Denominator errors:** paid-in vs committed; recycled/recallable capital. (3) **RVPI marks:** a deal-level "TVPI" that leans on an assumed exit multiple is a projection, not a realized figure — flag the unrealized share. (4) **Subscription-line inflation of IRR** is a fund-overlay issue, not deal-level, but note it if the deal IRR will feed a fund IRR. (5) Do not blend a gross deal IRR with a net fund IRR in the same comparison. |

## Item 3: Mid-Year Convention in LBO IRRs

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Convention** | HARD/ESTIMATE. Two schools: **(A) All flows mid-year** — every cash flow, including the exit/terminal proceeds, is placed at t−0.5 (interim FCF and exit both shift), reflecting that cash accrues throughout the year. **(B) Interim mid-year, exit at period-end** — operating/interim distributions shift to t−0.5, but the exit is treated as a discrete year-end (period-end) sale at t=N. School (B) is internally consistent with an exit-multiple sale, which is a **point-in-time** event assumed to occur at year-end. Mid-year treatment typically adds **~0.5 to 1.0 percentage point** to IRR versus year-end. The most rigorous practice avoids the schools debate entirely by using **XIRR against actual calendar close and exit dates** (with a stub period for the partial first year), which prices timing correctly without a convention toggle. |
| **Alternatives** | (a) **Year-end (end-period) convention**: standard for paper LBOs and the conservative default; Excel's `IRR`/`NPV` assume period-end. (b) **Mid-year via modified exponents** (cleanest), **uplift factor** (1+r)^0.5 on end-period PVs (fastest retrofit), or **XNPV/XIRR with mid-year dates** (best for irregular timing). (c) **Stub-period modeling**: multiply exit-year (or entry-year) flows by the fraction of year to align interest, D&A, and tax shields with a non-calendar close. |
| **Citations** | Macabacus (Enterprise & Equity Values): "The mid-period convention assumes that the UFCFs occur at the middle of each projection interval, while the end-period convention assumes all UFCFs occur at the end… the end-period convention is often used because it is more conservative… Note that the NPV function in Excel uses an end-period convention." Macabacus LBO stub-period guidance (income-statement items for the stub scaled by the fraction of the fiscal year remaining). Rosenbaum & Pearl (DCF chapter) present the mid-year discounting formula PV_t = FCF_t / (1+r)^(t−0.5). Practitioner consensus (Wall Street Prep; Sell Side Handbook LBO toggles: discounting equity flows to mid-year raises IRR via shorter discount timing; Wall Street Oasis: the exit-multiple method "uses year end discounting and assumes business is sold at the end of a year"). Delaware appraisal case law (*In re Sunbelt Beverage Corp. Shareholder Litigation*, 2010) cautions that using mid-year on operating cash flows but reverting to end-of-period on terminal value creates a defensibility problem on cross-examination. |
| **Reviewer-flag** | (1) **THE core inconsistency:** if the exit cash flow is pulled to t−0.5 (School A) while the **debt schedule still accrues a full year of interest** in the exit year, the model overstates exit-year interest (and understates exit equity) relative to the assumed half-year holding of the terminal proceeds. No mainstream source names this precise trap head-on, but it follows directly from the well-documented principle that an exit-multiple sale is a **year-end** event: full-year exit-year interest is only consistent with a **year-end exit (School B)**. Careful fixes: (i) keep the exit at period-end and accrue full-year interest (consistent); (ii) if exit is mid-year, run a **half-year interest stub** in the exit year with half-year D&A/tax; or (iii) use **XIRR on actual dates** so interest accrual and exit proceeds share the same clock. (2) **Consistency of convention** across operating flows and terminal value is the single most common error — apply one convention throughout. (3) `IRR` vs `XIRR`: `IRR` assumes equal annual spacing (wrong for real close/exit dates). (4) Disclose the convention: a +0.5–1.0 pt uplift purely from timing must be flagged to the IC, not presented as operational alpha. |

## Item 4: Management Incentive Structures — US vs UK/European

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Convention** | HARD (structure) / REPORTED (2024–2026 sizing). **US structure — management promote / incentive pool:** a pool of options / profits interests / restricted equity, typically **10–20% of fully diluted equity** (the Private Equity Toolkit standard: the total management equity award "typically represents about 15 to 20% of the company's common equity"). Participation is often **above a hurdle** (return of capital, sometimes a preferred return, or a MOIC/IRR threshold), with vesting commonly **~50% time-based (4–5 yrs) and ~50% performance-based**. Payout formula (profits-interest style): management proceeds = pool % × (exit equity value − hurdle threshold value), i.e., participation only on value above the hurdle; tiered ratchets increase the pool % as MOIC/IRR rises. **UK/European structure — institutional strip + sweet equity:** the sponsor (and rolling management) fund an **"institutional strip"** of shareholder loan notes / preference shares plus ordinary shares in a fixed ratio (commonly **9:1 up to 99:1** pref-to-ordinary; per Travers Smith 2025 the **most common coupon on shareholder debt is now 12%**, up from the traditional 10%). **Sweet equity** is a slice of the **ordinary** shares that management subscribes for **without** buying the accompanying preferred/loan notes, so 100% of their cash goes into ordinaries. Management proceeds = (management's ordinary %) × (exit equity value remaining after third-party debt + the institutional strip's loan notes/prefs + accrued coupon are repaid). The **envy ratio** = (investor £ per ordinary share) ÷ (management £ per ordinary share) = (investor investment ÷ investor % equity) ÷ (management investment ÷ management % equity). A ratchet can increase management's ordinary share above a MOIC/IRR hurdle. |
| **Alternatives** | US: **profits interests** (LLC, zero value at grant, capital-gains efficient), **options/RSUs/SARs**, **phantom equity**. Europe/UK: **growth shares / hurdle shares** (value only above a defined equity threshold — used where there is no preferred instrument; keeps upfront unrestricted market value low for tax), **ratchets** (positive or negative), pure **strip co-investment** (no sweet element). Hurdle basis differs: the European performance hurdle is often **implicit** in the ~10–12% preferred coupon (management ordinaries only pay after the strip is served), whereas US hurdles are **explicit** MOIC/IRR gates. |
| **Citations** | Travers Smith, *Private Equity Market Insights 2025* (Equity Trends): "The average sweet equity pot remained at 16% of the ordinary share capital… transactions at the smaller end… as high as 20-25%, while the largest deals may have a sweet equity pot size below 10%. The use of ratchets has continued to increase in 2024, with ratchets featuring on 55% of deals"; incremental ratchet equity above the hurdle **ranged 3.5%–20%**, typically with both a money/invested-capital multiple test and an IRR test; management reinvested **48%** of net sale proceeds (in line with post-2020 highs). Goodwin, *Incentive Equity: Vesting Criteria* (March 2024): "nearly two-thirds are conditioned on satisfaction of MOIC thresholds alone, approximately one-quarter are conditioned on satisfaction of IRR thresholds only, and the remaining plans include a combination of MOIC and IRR conditions"; ~62% of MIPs blend time- and performance-based vesting. Jamieson (US vs European MIPs): European sweet equity sits behind an 8–12% loan-note/pref coupon; US split ~50% time / ~50% performance; European pool larger because of the hurdle. Withers / Chambers / Forvis Mazars / Lexology (UK): institutional strip 9:1–99:1, sweet equity ~10–20% of ordinaries, CEO typically 3–5% (up to 8–10%), envy ratios commonly **2–5x**. CFI / Wall Street Oasis / Uría Menéndez: envy ratio formula and worked examples. Macabacus / A Simple Model / ibinterviewquestions: US pool 10–20% FD equity, hurdle MOIC tranches (e.g., +10% pool above 2.0x). |
| **Reviewer-flag** | (1) **DO NOT mix the two frameworks.** A US-style promote waterfall applied *on top of* a UK sweet-equity cap table **double-counts** management upside: in the UK model, management's disproportionate ordinary share **already** delivers the leverage that a US promote delivers via a separate carve-out. Layering a promote on the sweet-equity ordinaries rewards management twice for the same value above the strip. Pick the cap table that matches the deal's jurisdiction and model management proceeds **once**, through the actual instrument. (2) **Hurdle definition:** in the UK the preferred coupon *is* the hurdle — do not also impose a separate US MOIC gate unless the term sheet says so. (3) **Dilution timing:** model management on a **fully diluted** basis at exit; confirm whether the pool participates from the first dollar or only above a hurdle (materially different sponsor MOIC). (4) **Vesting/leaver treatment** affects the effective pool at exit (forfeiture reallocation). (5) **"10% management equity" is ambiguous** — 10% of ordinaries, 10% of FD equity above a hurdle, and 10% of exit proceeds after preference are not equivalent. (6) Show **payout curves** (downside/base/upside), not just the cap table. |

## Item 5: Monitoring / Management Fees (Portfolio Company → Sponsor)

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Convention** | HARD. In the **deal-level equity IRR**, monitoring/management fees paid **by the portfolio company to the sponsor** are an **operating cash outflow** at the company: they reduce EBITDA/FCF and therefore cash available for debt paydown and equity. They are often treated as an add-back for "adjusted EBITDA" (sponsor add-back) but are a **real cash cost** in the FCF and debt schedule. In a pure deal-level equity model, the fee is typically **not** also added to the sponsor's equity inflows — the sponsor's return is measured on distributions from the equity, not on the separate fee stream — **unless** the model explicitly consolidates sponsor-level economics, in which case the fee must appear as a sponsor inflow **and** as a company outflow (net ≈ zero at the consolidated sponsor level, minus tax and minority leakage). **Fund level:** monitoring/transaction fee income to the GP is subject to **management fee offset** provisions (ILPA best practice: 100% offset — fees charged to portfolio companies "should accrue to the benefit of the fund"). |
| **Alternatives** | (a) **Ignore/add-back entirely** (simplified models treat monitoring fees as non-operating and add them back to normalize EBITDA for valuation comparability). (b) **Model as a sponsor cash inflow** in a GP-economics or "sponsor total return" view (then must avoid double-counting). (c) **Accelerated / terminated monitoring fee at exit:** many monitoring fee agreements (MFAs) run 10+ years; on exit/IPO the sponsor can accelerate the **NPV of remaining fees** (lump sum, historically discounted at the risk-free rate). This reduces exit equity value available to all shareholders (a Use of exit proceeds) and is a sponsor inflow. Offset percentages vary: **100%** is the trend, but many funds historically used **75–80%** (or lower). |
| **Citations** | ILPA Private Equity Principles (GP Fee Income Offsets): "Transaction, monitoring, directory, advisory, exit fees, and other consideration charged by the GP should accrue to the benefit of the fund (i.e., 100% of transaction fees should be offset against management fees charged to the fund)." ILPA Reporting Template v2.0 (Jan 2025): itemized disclosure of monitoring fees, transaction fees, director fees and **fee offsets** (each offset itemized, not netted); accelerated monitoring fees explicitly named as a fee subject to offset. ILPA glossary "Management Fee Offsets." Winston & Strawn / ACG and Duane Morris: accelerated monitoring fee = lump sum of remaining monitoring fees on early exit, "discounted at the risk-free rate," an SEC enforcement focus on disclosure; where 100% offset applies, LP returns are unaffected, but 75–80% offsets leave residual GP income; excess fees at fund liquidation may be retained by the manager. |
| **Reviewer-flag** | (1) **Double-counting:** the classic error is counting the monitoring fee as a sponsor inflow **and** failing to deduct it from company FCF — or the reverse (deducting from FCF and also adding it back to EBITDA used for the exit multiple without adjusting cash). Be explicit: the fee is a wealth transfer from the company (all equityholders, including rollover/management) to the sponsor. (2) **Minority/rollover leakage:** if management or co-investors hold equity, sponsor fee extraction dilutes *their* return — a governance and alignment flag. (3) **Exit-value impact of accelerated fee:** the acceleration reduces net exit proceeds; confirm it is a Use at exit and that its NPV/discount rate is disclosed. (4) **Fund overlay:** if this deal IRR feeds a fund model, ensure the management-fee offset is applied so LP net returns are not overstated. (5) **Add-back scrutiny:** a reviewer will challenge a sponsor "adjusted EBITDA" that adds back monitoring fees at the exit multiple (it inflates exit EV) — the fee is real cash. |

## Item 6: Unlevered (Asset) IRR

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Convention** | HARD. The unlevered/asset IRR measures the return on the enterprise **independent of financing**. Cash flows: **entry** = full purchase enterprise value + fees that would exist regardless of leverage (advisory/legal/diligence), **excluding** financing-driven costs; **interim** = **unlevered free cash flow (UFCF / free cash flow to firm)** = **NOPAT + D&A − ΔNWC − capex**, where **NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − tax rate)**; **exit** = exit enterprise value (not equity value). No interest, no debt paydown, no financing fees. The defining feature: **taxes are computed on EBIT with no interest deduction** — taxes are therefore higher in the unlevered case because there is **no interest tax shield**. The unlevered IRR isolates operating value creation; the spread between the levered (pre-promote equity) IRR and the unlevered IRR is the contribution of leverage (financial engineering plus the tax shield). |
| **Alternatives** | (a) **APV-style separation**: value the unlevered cash flows at the unlevered cost of capital and add the PV of the tax shield separately (keeps operating and financing effects fully distinct). (b) **Pre-tax unlevered IRR** (some asset-return analyses strip tax entirely for cross-jurisdiction comparability). (c) **EBITDA-based proxies** for quick screens (ignoring capex/NWC/tax), which are not true UFCF. (d) Treatment of **cash taxes vs. book taxes** (NOLs, tax-basis step-ups under a 338(h)(10)/asset deal) can materially change the unlevered tax line. |
| **Citations** | Macabacus (DCF / UFCF and LBO value-creation): unlevered FCF built from NOPAT; leverage lowers WACC and drives equity value as debt is repaid — separating operating from financing value. Rosenbaum & Pearl (DCF chapter): UFCF = EBIT(1−t) + D&A − capex − increase in NWC; unlevered because it is "available to all capital providers" and taxes are computed on EBIT (no interest deduction). Wall Street Prep / CFI / BIWS: UFCF = NOPAT + D&A − ΔNWC − capex; "unlevered means no debt, no interest, no interest tax shield"; NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − tax rate). |
| **Reviewer-flag** | (1) **Tax-shield leakage:** the most common error is computing the unlevered IRR's taxes on EBT (after interest) or otherwise letting the interest tax shield sneak in — unlevered taxes must be on **EBIT**. (2) **Financing costs must be excluded:** financing fees, OID, commitment/revolver fees, and any debt-related cash flows do **not** belong in the asset IRR (they exist only because of leverage). (3) **Consistency of entry/exit basis:** entry EV and exit EV must be on the same (enterprise) basis — mixing exit equity value with entry EV breaks the asset IRR. (4) **Fees ambiguity:** advisory/legal fees are transaction costs that arguably belong in the asset return (they would be incurred in an all-equity deal); financing fees do not. State the treatment. (5) **Capex/D&A normalization** and maintenance-vs-growth capex assumptions drive UFCF — a reviewer will probe these. |

## Item 7: Value-Creation Bridges

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Convention** | HARD (components) / ESTIMATE (cross-term convention). Standard decomposition of equity value creation from entry to exit into: **(i) EBITDA growth** = ΔEBITDA × entry multiple (value from operating growth at the entry multiple); **(ii) multiple change / expansion** = ΔMultiple × exit (or entry) EBITDA (value from re-rating); **(iii) deleveraging / debt paydown** = the reduction in net debt over the hold (cash flow used to repay debt shifts EV from creditors to equity); **(iv) fees / other** = transaction and financing costs and other leakage (a drag). Standard identity: total equity value creation = ΔEnterprise Value + net debt paydown, where ΔEV splits into EBITDA growth, multiple change, and their **cross/interaction term** (ΔEBITDA × ΔMultiple). The bridge reconciles **entry equity check → exit equity value** (pre-promote). |
| **Alternatives** | (a) **Cross-term handling** — three schools: **(1) show it separately** as an explicit "interaction / combination" bar; **(2) allocate** it (e.g., split evenly, or fold it into EBITDA growth by using **exit** EBITDA in the multiple term and **entry** multiple in the EBITDA term); **(3) absorb** it (value EBITDA growth at the exit multiple, silently bundling the cross term into the growth bar). (b) **Finer operating splits**: EBITDA growth further decomposed into revenue growth, margin (gross/opex) expansion, and M&A/inorganic vs organic. (c) **Unlevering the value drivers** (AUXILIA / Puche 2016 approach): scale operating and multiple effects by the average equity ratio, isolating a separate **gearing/leverage** effect and a **cash-flow generation** effect. (d) **FCF/cash generation** shown as its own bar instead of, or alongside, debt paydown. |
| **Citations** | Rosenbaum & Pearl (LBO value creation): returns driven by EBITDA growth, multiple expansion, and debt paydown/deleveraging. Macabacus ("Creating Value in an LBO"): value from leverage/deleveraging plus operational improvements (organic growth, cost cutting, add-on M&A), and the caution that exit ≈ entry multiple is the prudent assumption. Wall Street Prep ("Returns Attribution / Value Creation"): EBITDA growth = ΔEBITDA × entry multiple; multiple expansion = ΔMultiple × exit EBITDA; worked bridge. Multiple Expansion (LBO Value Creation): Value Creation = ΔEnterprise Value + Debt Paydown; notes the operating-cash treatment inconsistency carried over from the LBO. AUXILIA Mathematica (Puche 2016 "Conventional/Derivative/Logarithmic" models): explicit cross/combination term and unlevered value drivers; VEG, VME, VCF, VGEAR components summing to ΔTEqV. Illustrative attribution ranges (practitioner): EBITDA growth ~40–60% of returns, debt paydown ~20–30%, multiple expansion ~10–30%. |
| **Reviewer-flag** | (1) **Reconcile to pre-promote equity first.** The bridge should explain total equity value creation **before** management incentive/carry; the management promote is a **distribution** of created value, not a driver of it. Show a separate "less: management incentive/promote" bar to get from pre-promote to sponsor equity. Building the bridge directly to post-promote equity conflates value creation with value **sharing**. (2) **Cross-term transparency:** silently absorbing the ΔEBITDA × ΔMultiple interaction into the growth or multiple bar overstates one lever; a reviewer will ask where the interaction went. State the convention. (3) **Multiple-expansion honesty:** an assumed exit multiple > entry multiple is usually the least defensible lever; flag it as an assumption, not value created. (4) **Operating-cash / net-debt consistency:** the debt-paydown bar must use the same net-debt definition as the returns section. (5) **Fees bar:** ensure transaction/financing fees and monitoring-fee leakage are captured so the bridge actually ties to the model's exit equity. (6) **Leverage effect:** if the audience expects an "unlevered value creation" view, the conventional levered bridge overstates the EBITDA-growth and multiple contributions — consider the unlevered/gearing split. |

## Recommendations (staged, deal-level)

1. **Lock the Sources & Uses and fee logic first.** Put advisory fees (expensed), financing fees (contra-liability, amortized to interest), and OID (contra-liability, accreted to interest) in Uses; raise debt at face; let sponsor equity plug. Net rollover against the sponsor check and compute MOIC on the sponsor-only equity. **Trigger to revisit:** if fees are debt-funded or the deal is CFDF, rebuild the bridge.
2. **Adopt "pre-promote IRR" labeling and define it once.** State it is net of transaction costs and portfolio-company fees but before management incentive/promote and before any fund-level fees/carry. Keep a parallel **unlevered (asset) IRR** and a **post-promote equity IRR**. Never use the word "gross" for the deal series. **Change trigger:** if the model will feed a fund/LP report, add a net-to-LP layer and reconcile to ILPA/GIPS definitions.
3. **Choose ONE timing convention and enforce it.** Preferred: **XIRR on actual close/exit dates with a first-year stub**, so interest accrual and exit proceeds share one clock. If you use an integer mid-year convention, either keep the exit at period-end with full-year exit-year interest (consistent) or run a half-year interest/D&A/tax stub in the exit year. Disclose the +0.5–1.0 pt timing effect to the IC.
4. **Match the incentive structure to the jurisdiction and model it once.** US promote-pool waterfall **or** UK institutional-strip + sweet-equity cap table — never both. Use 2024–2026 sizing as sanity checks (US pool 10–20% FD equity; UK sweet equity 16% of ordinary share capital on average, 20–25% on smaller deals, below 10% on the largest, ratchets on 55% of deals; hurdles commonly ~2–3x MOIC and/or ~20% IRR; envy ratios ~2–5x; shareholder-debt coupon most commonly 12%). Show downside/base/upside payout curves.
5. **Handle monitoring fees explicitly and avoid double-counting.** Deduct from company FCF; only add to sponsor inflows in a consolidated sponsor-economics view. Model accelerated monitoring-fee NPV as a Use at exit if applicable. If a fund overlay follows, apply the management-fee offset (target 100%).
6. **Build the value bridge to pre-promote equity, show the cross term, and flag multiple expansion.** Add a separate promote/management-incentive bar to reach sponsor equity. Keep net-debt definitions consistent with the returns section.

## Caveats
- **2024–2026 sizing figures are REPORTED** market data from law-firm/adviser publications (Travers Smith *PE Market Insights 2025*, Goodwin, Jamieson, Withers, Chambers, Forvis Mazars) and reflect mid-to-upper European or US mid-market samples; individual deals vary widely by size, sector, and sponsor nationality. The Travers Smith figures (16% average sweet equity, 55% of deals with ratchets, 12% coupon, 48% management reinvestment, 3.5–20% ratchet band) are drawn from its 2024 UK/European deal sample; US pool figures (10–20% FD equity) come from practitioner references (Private Equity Toolkit, Macabacus). Value-creation lever attribution ranges are illustrative rules of thumb, not universal.
- **The specific mid-year-exit vs full-year-interest inconsistency is a synthesis/analytical point:** no top-tier source (Macabacus, Rosenbaum & Pearl) names it head-on. The supporting logic rests on the well-documented principle that an exit-multiple sale is a year-end event and on Delaware appraisal guidance against mixing conventions.
- **Accounting treatment (Item 1)** reflects US GAAP post-ASU 2015-03; IFRS and pre-2015 models differ (financing fees historically a capitalized asset amortized on a straight-line basis).
- **"Gross IRR" usage** is defined slightly differently by ILPA (before mgmt fees and carry), GIPS (net of transaction costs, before carry and mgmt fee), and deal practitioners (before promote) — always specify the deduction stack.
- Several supporting citations are secondary (training providers, forums) corroborating primary ILPA/GIPS/Macabacus/Rosenbaum & Pearl sources; where only secondary sources exist (e.g., the accelerated-fee discount rate), findings are tagged REPORTED.

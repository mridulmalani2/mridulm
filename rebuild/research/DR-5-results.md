# LBO Output Exhibit Conventions for PE Investment Committee Presentations

## TL;DR
- Across authoritative sources (Rosenbaum & Pearl, Wall Street Prep, Macabacus, Multiple Expansion, CFI, and real banker decks such as Goldman Sachs' J.Crew materials), LBO IC exhibits follow a stable convention set: a top-loaded one-page transaction summary (returns → sources & uses → capitalization/credit stats → FCF), paired IRR-and-multiple sensitivity data tables, entry-fixed operating scenarios (base/upside/downside), a three-lever value-creation bridge (EBITDA growth, multiple change, debt paydown), and a cash-generation/deleveraging schedule.
- The single most important structural convention: **entry price and deal structure are held fixed within operating cases** — only operating performance and exit vary; re-pricing entry happens in separate sensitivity/financing-structure tables, not within a downside case. This is what lets the IC isolate operational risk from valuation risk.
- Design your tool's outputs to mirror these six standardized exhibits, defaulting exit multiple = entry multiple, showing IRR and MOIC together, decomposing value creation net of fees, and footnoting simplifications (annual periods, static rates) on a dedicated assumptions page rather than burying them.

## Key Findings
The six exhibits below are each presented as a standalone table describing the standard exhibit, its conventions, and cited practitioner examples. Conventions are consistent across large-cap and lower-middle-market deals in structure, but diverge in detail (leverage levels, covenant type, growth-vs-engineering emphasis), which is noted per item.

## Details

### Item 1 — The LBO One-Pager / Transaction Summary Exhibit

| Aspect | Standard convention | Cited examples / sources |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Single-page "transaction summary" that lets an IC grasp the deal at a glance: price, financing, returns, credit. | Rosenbaum & Pearl Step V(d) "Create Transaction Summary Page" (Ch. 5, p. 261); the assembled "Illustrative LBO Analysis for ValueCo" (p. 262). |
| Information hierarchy (top→bottom) | (1) Purchase Price / entry multiples; (2) Sources & Uses; (3) Return Analysis (IRR + Cash Return/MOIC); (4) Capitalization (tranche-by-tranche with cumulative x EBITDA and % of total cap); (5) Credit Statistics; (6) Free Cash Flow panel. R&P combine all of these panels on one page. | R&P ValueCo Transaction Summary Page multi-panel layout: Sources/Uses + Purchase Price + Return Analysis + Capitalization + Credit Statistics + FCF. |
| Sources side line items | Revolving Credit Facility, Term Loan A/B/C, 2nd Lien, Senior Notes, Senior Subordinated Notes, Equity Contribution, Rollover Equity, Cash on Hand → Total Sources. Columns: Amount, % of Total Sources, Multiple of EBITDA (with running "Cumulative" leverage column), Pricing (spread/coupon). | R&P "LBO Analysis – Template"; Macabacus S&U (rolled-over debt/equity appears as both a source and a use). |
| Uses side line items | Purchase Equity, Repay Existing Debt, Tender/Call Premiums, Financing Fees, Other Fees & Expenses → Total Uses. Sources must equal Uses to the dollar. | R&P template; Wall Street Prep; CFI. Equity Contribution is the "plug" (Total Uses less all other sources). |
| Returns block | IRR and MOIC (R&P call the money multiple "Cash Return," e.g., 2.5x) shown together, prominently. Sponsors report both because MOIC shows magnitude and IRR shows speed/time value. Per Wall Street Prep, "the minimum internal rate of return (IRR) is usually 20% for most private equity firms," and per Ryan O'Connell, CFA, sponsors "historically target gross IRRs of 20-25% or higher for control buyouts." | R&P Ch. 4 "Returns Analysis—Internal Rate of Return" (p.181) and "—Cash Return" (p.182); Wall Street Prep; ibinterviewquestions; Ryan O'Connell. |
| Credit statistics block | Total Debt/EBITDA, Senior/1st-lien Debt/EBITDA, % Debt/Total Cap, EBITDA/Total Interest, (EBITDA–Capex)/Cash Interest, shown for LTM and each projection year. | R&P Credit Statistics block; Goldman J.Crew deck credit stats (Total Debt/EBITDA, Adj. Debt/EBITDAR, EBITDA/Int, EBITDA-Capex/Int by year). |
| Divergence by deal type | Large-cap: multiple tranches (TLA/TLB/2nd lien/notes), covenant-lite. Lower-middle-market: simpler structure (one term loan + revolver, seller notes), often maintenance covenants, lower leverage. Per PGIM (as of Sept 30, 2024, cited in ABF Journal), middle-market first-lien debt averaged **4.5x** leverage (debt/EBITDA) vs **5.8x** in the broadly syndicated loan market. | ABF Journal / PGIM data; CT Acquisitions covenant-lite TLB note. |

### Item 2 — Sensitivity Tables (Data Tables)

| Aspect | Standard convention | Cited examples / sources |
|---|---|---|
| Standard axes | Most common: Entry multiple × Exit multiple; and Leverage (Debt/EBITDA) × Exit multiple; also Exit multiple × Exit year; and (for ops-driven deals) Revenue growth × EBITDA margin, or EBITDA growth × Exit multiple. | ibinterviewquestions ("sensitize entry multiple against exit multiple and leverage against exit multiple, measured against IRR"); CFI; R&P (Entry/Exit multiple, Exit year axes). |
| Grid size | Typically 5×5 (some 3×3 to 7×7). R&P house style is ~5-wide two-variable tables (DCF analog: WACC 10–12% × exit multiple 6.5x–8.5x). | Ryan O'Connell 5×5 grid; R&P; Multiple Expansion (entry/exit 8.0x–10.0x groups of five). |
| Base-case highlighting | Base case placed at grid center; conditional formatting (green ≥20% IRR / yellow 15–20% / red <15%) highlights the band that clears the fund's target return. Per Wall Street Prep the PE minimum IRR "is usually 20%," and control-buyout targets run "20-25% or higher" (Ryan O'Connell). | uplevered; ibinterviewquestions; Ryan O'Connell green/yellow/red. |
| IRR + MOIC together | Standard to show paired tables — one IRR grid and one MOIC/Cash-Return grid over the same axes. | Multiple Expansion ("MoIC sensitivity table and an IRR sensitivity table"); R&P parallel IRR and Cash Return grids. |
| Practitioner caveat | Revenue-CAGR/margin sensitivities are "highly illustrative" and harder to wire when the P&L is driven by detailed operating assumptions; entry/exit multiple tables are the clean standard. | Wall Street Oasis practitioner threads. |
| Excel mechanic | Two-variable Data Tables (Data > What-If Analysis > Data Table); requires live references back to assumption cells. | CT Acquisitions; Multiple Expansion "Data Tables the Right Way." |

### Item 3 — Downside / Base / Upside Operating Case Construction

| Aspect | Standard convention | Cited examples / sources |
|---|---|---|
| What is shocked | Operating variables: revenue growth (volume/price), EBITDA margin, cost savings/synergies, capex, working capital. Cases: Management (often = optimistic), Upside, Base, Downside ("kill the business in 2–3 of the 5 years"). | Wall Street Oasis ("Downside - kill the business in 2-3 of the 5 years"); Wall Street Prep; R&P Operating Scenarios (Base + upside/downside). |
| **Entry held fixed (critical convention)** | **Entry price, entry multiple, leverage, and Sources & Uses are held CONSTANT across operating cases.** Only operating performance and (sometimes) exit vary. Re-pricing entry/leverage is a separate financing-structure or sensitivity exercise, kept apart so drivers of IRR can be cleanly decomposed. | Wall Street Oasis (best practice: "systematically hold one of those scenarios constant… for a given financing scenario, what happens when operating assumptions are positive, base, negative…"); R&P separate "Financing Structure 1/2/3" from "Operating Scenario Base/Up/Down." |
| Why | Mixing financing and operating flexes makes it impossible to attribute IRR/cash-flow changes; the IC needs to see operational risk against a fixed purchase decision (the price they're being asked to approve). | Wall Street Oasis; conventional attribution logic. |
| Exit within cases | Base case convention: exit multiple = entry multiple (multiple expansion treated as upside only). Downside may also compress exit multiple; some firms flex exit multiple with the operating case. | R&P ValueCo (exit = entry = 8.0x); Wall Street Prep; Ryan O'Connell. |
| Credit outcomes in downside | Present alongside equity returns: leverage (Net Debt/EBITDA) through the hold, min interest/fixed-charge coverage, DSCR, covenant compliance flags (breach years), cash-sweep behavior, revolver draws, minimum-liquidity/negative-cash checks. Downside case is "of particular importance" because of the debt load. | Wall Street Prep advanced test (maintenance-covenant compliance checks); agentskills.finance ("credit metrics dashboard: leverage/coverage/covenant compliance by year," "flag covenant breaches"); investmentbankinganalysts (springing covenants, sweep/revolver interaction). |
| Divergence by deal type | Growth equity: less leverage, downside centers on growth miss/cash burn, covenants less central. Classic buyout: downside centers on covenant/DSCR breach and liquidity. | General practitioner consensus. |

### Item 4 — Value-Creation Bridge / Attribution Analysis

| Aspect | Standard convention | Cited examples / sources |
|---|---|---|
| What it shows | Decomposes equity value change from entry to exit into: EBITDA growth, multiple expansion/contraction, debt paydown (deleveraging); sometimes FCF/cash generation broken out separately. | Wall Street Prep Returns Attribution; CFI; Multiple Expansion; Nasdaq eVestment valuation bridge. |
| Standard bar order (waterfall) | Start at Entry Equity Value → + EBITDA growth → +/- Multiple expansion → + Debt paydown → arrive at Exit Equity Value. (EBITDA growth first as the operational lever, then multiple, then deleveraging.) | uplevered ("start at entry equity value, then a bar for EBITDA growth, another for multiple expansion, another for debt paydown, arriving at exit equity value"); Mosaic MOIC Decomp. |
| Standard formulas | EBITDA Growth = (Exit EBITDA − Entry EBITDA) × entry multiple; Multiple Expansion = (Exit multiple − Entry multiple) × exit EBITDA; Debt Paydown = Entry Net Debt − Exit Net Debt. | Wall Street Prep (EBITDA growth ×entry multiple; multiple ×exit EBITDA); CFI; Mosaic. |
| Interaction / cross term | The ΔEBITDA×ΔMultiple cross term is real. Convention: implicitly absorbed by the ordering choice (using entry multiple for the growth term and exit EBITDA for the multiple term folds the cross term into the multiple bar). Academically it can be shown as a separate "combination" bar (ΔmΔC). | Christoph Jäckel (explicit ΔmΔC combination component); Wall Street Prep convention embeds it via formula choice. |
| Gross vs net of fees | Best practice: bridge net of fees — add transaction/financing fees to entry equity and subtract fees from exit proceeds so entry and exit are treated consistently. Many quick high-level LBOs omit exit fees. Carry/management fees are usually excluded from the deal-level (gross) bridge; a separate gross-to-net step handles LP economics. | Multiple Expansion ("This value creation analysis is net of fees… any money the sponsor receives comes after paying down debt and transaction fees"); Wall Street Prep (fees added to entry EV, subtracted from exit EV). |
| MOIC-basis presentation | Industry standard to also divide each dollar component by entry equity to state attribution on a MOIC basis. | Mosaic MOIC Decomp. |
| Divergence by deal type | Financial-engineering deals (flat EBITDA/flat multiple): ~100% of return from debt paydown, capping ~1.7–2.0x MOIC. Ops-driven deals increasingly rely on EBITDA growth: per the McKinsey 2024 Global Private Markets Review (cited by CT Acquisitions), EBITDA growth "would need to contribute 55 to 65 percent of total value creation in 2024 to 2027 vintage deals, up from 47 percent in 2010 to 2022." | CT Acquisitions / McKinsey attribution ranges. |

### Item 5 — FCF / Deleveraging Exhibit

| Aspect | Standard convention | Cited examples / sources |
|---|---|---|
| Line items | EBITDA → less Cash Interest → less Capex → less/plus ΔWorking Capital → less Taxes → less Mandatory Amortization → Free Cash Flow (for debt paydown); then Cumulative FCF. | R&P FCF panel (EBITDA − Cash Interest − Capex → FCF/Cumulative FCF, with "% of Bank Debt Repaid"); Goldman J.Crew "FCF For Debt Paydown" build (Net Income + D&A + fin. fees amort. − Capex − ΔOWC). |
| Key subtotals | (1) FCF conversion % = FCF/EBITDA; (2) Cumulative debt paid down as % of entry/bank debt; (3) Net Debt/EBITDA by year (deleveraging path). Per ibinterviewquestions, "a well-structured LBO… might pay down 30-40% of initial debt through mandatory amortization and cash sweeps combined over a 5-year hold." | R&P "% of Bank Debt Repaid"; Wall Street Prep (cumulative FCF = total debt paydown when 100% sweep); CFI; ibinterviewquestions. |
| Deleveraging schedule layout | Rows across the hold: Total Debt (and by tranche), Total Cash, Net Debt, and Total Debt/EBITDA + Adj. leverage falling each year. | Goldman J.Crew "De-leveraging Profile" (Bank Debt/Senior Notes/Total Debt + Total Cash by year) and Credit Statistics (Total Debt/EBITDA 2.3x→0.0x). |
| Standard modeling assumption | 100% of FCF sweeps to debt in basic models; cumulative FCF until exit ≈ total debt paydown. Real models use TLB mandatory amortization (the LSTA standard 1% annual with a bullet at maturity, per ibinterviewquestions) plus a cash-sweep waterfall — per LSTA cash-sweep data (cited by CT Acquisitions), "the standard step-down structure starts at 75 percent sweep when leverage is above 4.5x and steps to 50 percent at 3.5 to 4.5x and 0 percent below 3.5x." | Street of Walls paper LBO; Wall Street Prep cash sweep; CT Acquisitions / LSTA; ibinterviewquestions. |
| Divergence by deal type | Asset-light/high-FCF-conversion businesses show strong deleveraging; capex-heavy/cyclical show weaker. Middle-market deals emphasize the deleveraging engine more (less multiple expansion available). | Street of Walls LBO candidate characteristics; ABF Journal middle-market data. |

### Item 6 — Disclosing Methodology Limitations & Simplifying Assumptions

| Aspect | Standard convention | Cited examples / sources |
|---|---|---|
| What gets caveated | Annual (not quarterly/monthly) periods; static/constant interest rates; constant tax rate; no working-capital seasonality; all debt paydown at period-end (to avoid circularity); simplified/constant transaction fees; exit multiple = entry as a conservative convention. | Street of Walls (annual period assumption; "assume all debt pay-down occurs at moment of sale… eliminates circular dependency"); BIWS (constant transaction fees; annual IRR limitations); O'Reilly ch. "Assumptions of LBO Models." |
| Where disclosed | Dedicated "Key Assumptions" page and/or footnotes to each exhibit — not buried. Each material input listed with source (or a [VERIFY] flag). Model header states operating scenario, financing structure, currency, fiscal-year convention. | agentskills.finance ("Key assumptions page listing every material input with source"); R&P model header ("Operating Scenario: Base / $ in millions / fiscal year ending…"); Goldman J.Crew footnotes (LIBOR floor, financing-fee %, tax rate, rent capitalization multiple). |
| Professional tone convention | State simplifications matter-of-factly as scope choices, not apologies; pair each with why it's immaterial or conservative. Don't minimize real risks — "IC members will find them anyway; credibility matters." Sensitive/qualitative characterizations handled verbally (decks are discoverable in litigation). | LobeHub/financecontext IC-memo skill ("Don't minimize risks… credibility matters"); KKR IC guide (sensitive topics discussed verbally, not in deck); educational disclaimers (Ryan O'Connell "illustrative… may not reflect current market conditions"). |
| Framing | Present the base case as one point in a range; sensitivity + scenario tables are themselves the primary "caveat" mechanism — they demonstrate the modeler understands which assumptions move the answer. | ibinterviewquestions ("a model is a range, not a point"); Addepar/Stanford memo research (scenarios let IC reason about upside/downside). |
| Divergence by deal type | Large-cap/institutional: fuller assumptions appendix, QoE-adjusted EBITDA disclosures. LMM/independent sponsor: shorter, but still an assumptions page; heavier reliance on management case with explicit adjustments noted. | Wall Street Oasis (management vs base case + QoE adjustments); deliverables.ai memo structure. |

## Recommendations
1. **Build the one-pager to the R&P panel order and make it the default landing exhibit**: Purchase Price/entry multiples → Sources & Uses (with % of total and cumulative x-EBITDA columns) → Returns (IRR + MOIC side by side) → Capitalization → Credit Statistics → FCF. This matches what ICs at both bulge-bracket-advised and sponsor shops expect.
2. **Ship paired 5×5 sensitivity tables** (IRR and MOIC) on Entry×Exit multiple and Leverage×Exit multiple, base case centered, green/yellow/red banding at the fund's IRR hurdle (default the threshold to ~20%, adjustable to a 20–25% band). Let users toggle a third axis (exit year, EBITDA growth).
3. **Enforce the entry-fixed scenario architecture**: separate "Operating Scenario" (base/upside/downside — flex only operating drivers) from "Financing Structure" (flex leverage/S&U). Never let a downside case silently re-price entry. Surface a credit dashboard (leverage, coverage, DSCR, covenant-breach flags, sweep/revolver) beside downside equity returns.
4. **Default the value-creation bridge** to Entry Equity → EBITDA growth → multiple change → debt paydown → Exit Equity, net of transaction/financing fees, with a MOIC-basis view and an optional separate cross-term bar for rigor.
5. **Make deleveraging first-class**: show FCF conversion (FCF/EBITDA), cumulative debt paid as % of entry debt, and Net Debt/EBITDA by year; wire the cash-sweep default to the LSTA step-down (75% above 4.5x, 50% at 3.5–4.5x, 0% below 3.5x) with 1% TLB mandatory amortization.
6. **Add a structured Assumptions page** with per-input sourcing and a footnote library (annual periods, static rates, constant tax, period-end paydown, exit=entry). Frame as scope choices with brief rationale.
- **Benchmarks that change the design**: for LMM/independent-sponsor users, default to simpler single-tranche S&U, maintenance-covenant tracking, and lower leverage presets — per PGIM (June 2024), "lower mid-market transactions generally range between 3.5x to 4.5x EBITDA." For upper-mid/large-cap, default to multi-tranche covenant-lite structures — PGIM notes "upper mid-market buyouts typically secure financing at 5.0x to 5.5x EBITDA" — with incurrence-covenant framing.

## Caveats
- Some sources are practitioner/educational (Wall Street Oasis threads, course marketing pages) rather than primary; I corroborated conventions across multiple independent sources and anchored to primary artifacts (R&P template, Goldman J.Crew SEC filing) where possible.
- Exact R&P dollar figures differ between editions (2nd ed. fiscal 2008-dated; 3rd ed. 2012-dated) and come partly from user-uploaded copies of the model template; the exhibit architecture, row/column labels, axes, and exit=entry 8.0x convention are firmly corroborated, but treat individual dollar values as approximate.
- The Goldman J.Crew example is a recapitalization/dividend analysis (not a full sponsor buyout), but its Sources & Uses, FCF-for-debt-paydown, deleveraging profile, and credit-statistics panels illustrate the same banker exhibit conventions relevant to an LBO one-pager.
- "Net of fees" in value bridges refers to transaction/financing fees at the deal level; LP-level gross-to-net (management fees and carry) is a separate fund-economics step generally not shown in the deal-level bridge.
- Leverage benchmarks (PGIM/ABF Journal, McKinsey, LSTA) reflect 2024 market conditions and shift with the credit cycle; treat presets as defaults to be re-tuned to current markets, not fixed rules.
